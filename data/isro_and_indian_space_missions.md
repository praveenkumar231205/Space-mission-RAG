# ISRO and Indian Space Missions

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) was established on August 15, 1969 and is headquartered in Bengaluru. India's first satellite, Aryabhata, was launched in April 1975 on a Soviet rocket. Rakesh Sharma became the first Indian in space in April 1984 aboard a Soviet Soyuz spacecraft.

## Launch vehicles

ISRO's Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV) launched 104 satellites in a single mission in February 2017, a record at the time. The heavier LVM3 rocket launches larger satellites and the Chandrayaan-3 lunar mission. Launches take place from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota.

## Moon and Mars

Chandrayaan-1, launched in October 2008, helped confirm the presence of water molecules on the Moon. Mangalyaan, also called the Mars Orbiter Mission, launched on November 5, 2013 and entered Mars orbit on September 24, 2014. India became the first nation to reach Mars orbit on its first attempt, at a cost of about 450 crore rupees (roughly 74 million US dollars).

Chandrayaan-3 launched on July 14, 2023. Its lander Vikram touched down near the lunar south pole on August 23, 2023, carrying the rover Pragyan. India became the fourth country to soft-land on the Moon, after the Soviet Union, the United States and China, and the first to land near the south polar region.

## Sun, docking and human spaceflight

Aditya-L1, India's first solar observatory, launched on September 2, 2023 and reached its halo orbit around the Sun-Earth Lagrange point L1 in January 2024. In January 2025, the SpaDeX mission demonstrated docking of two small satellites, making India the fourth country with this capability.

Gaganyaan is India's human spaceflight program. It aims to send a crew of three to low Earth orbit at about 400 kilometers altitude for up to three days. In mid-2025, Group Captain Shubhanshu Shukla flew to the International Space Station on the commercial Axiom-4 mission, becoming the first Indian astronaut to visit the station.
