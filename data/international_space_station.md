# International Space Station

The International Space Station (ISS) is a large research laboratory in low Earth orbit. It is a partnership between five space agencies: NASA (United States), Roscosmos (Russia), ESA (Europe), JAXA (Japan) and the Canadian Space Agency.

## Construction and occupation

The first module, Zarya, was launched in November 1998. Astronauts and cosmonauts have lived aboard continuously since November 2, 2000. The station was assembled in orbit over more than a decade by a series of Space Shuttle and Russian rocket flights.

## Orbit and size

The ISS orbits at an altitude of roughly 400 kilometers and travels at about 28,000 kilometers per hour. It completes an orbit about every 90 minutes, so crews see around 16 sunrises and sunsets each day. The station is about 109 meters long, roughly the length of a football field, and has a mass of about 420,000 kilograms.

## Research and future

Experiments aboard the ISS study microgravity effects on human health, materials, combustion and biology. The station is expected to be retired around 2030 to 2031. NASA has selected SpaceX to build a vehicle that will guide the station to a controlled reentry over a remote part of the Pacific Ocean. Commercial space stations are planned to succeed it.
