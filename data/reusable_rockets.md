# Reusable Rockets

Traditional rockets are discarded after a single flight. Reusable rockets recover and refly major parts, which lowers the cost of reaching space.

## Space Shuttle

The Space Shuttle was partially reusable. Its orbiters flew 135 missions between 1981 and 2011, but the external tank was discarded each flight and refurbishing the orbiters was expensive.

## Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy

Blue Origin's New Shepard suborbital rocket made the first vertical landing of a reusable rocket on November 23, 2015. On December 21, 2015, SpaceX landed a Falcon 9 first stage after an orbital launch for the first time. The first reflight of an orbital-class booster came on March 30, 2017 with the SES-10 mission. SpaceX's Falcon Heavy first launched on February 6, 2018, carrying a Tesla Roadster, and its side boosters landed back at Cape Canaveral.

## Starship

Starship is SpaceX's fully reusable two-stage launch system, made of the Super Heavy booster and the Starship upper stage. Stacked, it stands roughly 120 meters tall. The first integrated flight test on April 20, 2023 ended in an explosion. On October 13, 2024, during the fifth flight test, the Super Heavy booster returned to the launch tower and was caught by its mechanical arms.

## Others

Blue Origin's heavy-lift New Glenn rocket first flew in January 2025 and is designed with a reusable first stage. Rocket Lab's Electron has also recovered boosters from the ocean.
