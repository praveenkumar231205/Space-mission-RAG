# Apollo Program

The Apollo program was NASA's human spaceflight effort to land astronauts on the Moon. It ran from 1961 to 1972. In a speech to Congress on May 25, 1961, President John F. Kennedy set the goal of landing a man on the Moon and returning him safely to Earth before the end of the decade.

## Apollo 11

Apollo 11 launched on July 16, 1969. On July 20, 1969, the lunar module Eagle landed in the Sea of Tranquility, carrying Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin. Armstrong became the first person to step onto the lunar surface, followed by Aldrin. Michael Collins stayed in lunar orbit aboard the command module Columbia. The crew splashed down in the Pacific Ocean on July 24, 1969.

## Rockets and landings

All crewed lunar missions launched on the Saturn V rocket, which stood about 110 meters (363 feet) tall. There were six successful crewed Moon landings: Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16 and 17. In total, twelve astronauts walked on the Moon. The last landing, Apollo 17 in December 1972, was commanded by Eugene Cernan. The missions returned about 382 kilograms of lunar rock and soil samples to Earth.

## Setbacks

On January 27, 1967, a cabin fire during a launch rehearsal for Apollo 1 killed astronauts Gus Grissom, Ed White and Roger Chaffee. In April 1970, an oxygen tank explosion on Apollo 13 forced the crew to abort their landing. They returned safely to Earth using the lunar module as a lifeboat.
